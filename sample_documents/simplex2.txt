TITLE: Simplex 2 — Acetaminophen, COX Pathways, and Postoperative Delirium: Rethinking Analgesic Mechanisms Through an SPM Lens

ABSTRACT:
Postoperative delirium (POD) affects up to 50% of elderly surgical patients and carries significant mortality risk. Current mechanistic models focus on neuroinflammation via IL-6, TNF-α, and blood-brain barrier disruption. This paper proposes a complementary model: perioperative depletion of SPMs — specifically resolvins and protectins — due to surgical trauma and conventional analgesic regimens, creates a resolution failure state that manifests as POD. We examine acetaminophen's paradoxical role: its COX inhibition may interfere with aspirin-triggered SPM biosynthesis pathways while its central TRPV1 mechanisms may be neuroprotective.

INTRODUCTION:
The neuroinflammatory basis of POD is established. What remains underexplored is the resolution side of this equation. Surgical trauma triggers a massive eicosanoid surge; standard perioperative analgesia (NSAIDs, opioids, acetaminophen) modulates this surge in ways that may inadvertently suppress SPM generation. This paper positions POD not as excess inflammation, but as inflammation that cannot resolve.

ACETAMINOPHEN MECHANISMS:
Acetaminophen (APAP) is not a simple COX inhibitor. Its mechanisms include: (1) inhibition of a COX-3 splice variant in the CNS, (2) activation of the descending serotonergic pain pathway, (3) endocannabinoid system modulation via FAAH inhibition — increasing anandamide levels, (4) TRPV1 desensitization via its active metabolite AM404. The AM404 pathway is clinically significant: it links acetaminophen to both analgesic efficacy and potential neuroprotection via CB1 receptor activation in the periaqueductal gray.

SPM DEPLETION HYPOTHESIS:
Surgical trauma → COX-2 upregulation → prostaglandin surge → aspirin-modified COX-2 becomes unavailable for SPM synthesis → E-series resolvin production drops → neuroinflammation lacks resolution signals → microglial activation persists → POD. Acetaminophen's COX inhibition may compound this by further reducing substrate availability for lipid mediator class switching.

CLINICAL CORRELATES:
Epidemiological data show: patients receiving high omega-3 perioperative nutrition have reduced POD incidence (indirect SPM support). Statins (which upregulate lipoxin biosynthesis) reduce POD risk. Dexamethasone reduces POD but may impair SPM-mediated tissue repair. These patterns are consistent with, though do not prove, the SPM depletion model.

PROPOSED MECHANISM MAP:
Surgical stress → IL-6, TNF-α surge AND SPM biosynthesis failure → dual pathways to microglial hyperactivation → neuroinflammation without resolution → cognitive dysfunction phenotype (POD).

GAPS:
1. No direct measurement of perioperative SPM levels in POD vs non-POD patients exists in literature.
2. The relative contribution of SPM depletion vs pro-inflammatory excess to POD remains unquantified.
3. Acetaminophen's net effect on perioperative SPM biosynthesis is unknown.
4. Animal models of POD rarely measure SPM levels in brain tissue.

CONCLUSION:
Simplex 2 establishes the resolution failure hypothesis for POD. Simplex 3 will develop the logarithmic pain perception model that may explain why SPM-deficient states amplify pain signaling beyond what pro-inflammatory mediators alone would predict.
