TITLE: Simplex 1 — Specialized Pro-Resolving Mediators in Neuroinflammation: A Framework for Resolution Biology

ABSTRACT:
Specialized pro-resolving mediators (SPMs) represent a class of lipid-derived signaling molecules that actively terminate inflammation rather than simply suppressing it. This paper establishes the foundational framework for the Simplex series, arguing that resolution of neuroinflammation is a biosynthetically active, receptor-mediated process. We introduce the SPM triad — resolvins, protectins, and maresins — and map their biosynthetic origins from omega-3 fatty acid precursors (EPA and DHA). The central hypothesis: failure of SPM biosynthesis, rather than excess pro-inflammatory signaling alone, underlies chronic neuroinflammatory conditions.

INTRODUCTION:
Inflammation resolution has historically been treated as passive — the mere cessation of pro-inflammatory signals. This view is fundamentally incomplete. Active resolution involves coordinated lipid mediator class switching, from pro-inflammatory eicosanoids (prostaglandins, leukotrienes) to pro-resolving lipoxins, resolvins, protectins, and maresins. This series (Simplex 1–5) will build a unified mechanistic model linking SPM biology to clinical outcomes including postoperative delirium, chronic pain, and neurodegeneration.

MECHANISMS:
Resolvin D1 (RvD1) is biosynthesized from DHA via sequential 15-LOX and 5-LOX activity, producing 17-HDHA as an intermediate. RvD1 binds FPR2/ALX receptors on neutrophils, macrophages, and microglia, triggering: (1) halt of further neutrophil infiltration, (2) enhancement of efferocytosis, (3) suppression of NF-κB signaling without immunosuppression. Resolvin E1 (RvE1) follows the EPA pathway via aspirin-modified COX-2, demonstrating that anti-inflammatory drugs can redirect rather than simply block lipid mediator biosynthesis.

SPM TRIAD:
Resolvins (D-series from DHA, E-series from EPA): primary mediators of neutrophil clearance and macrophage reprogramming. Protectin D1 (PD1/NPD1): neuroprotective; inhibits T cell migration; critical in retinal and brain tissue. Maresins (from DHA via 14-LOX): macrophage-specific; promote tissue regeneration; least studied of the triad.

GAPS IN CURRENT LITERATURE:
1. Most SPM research uses supraphysiological concentrations not achievable clinically.
2. SPM receptor polymorphisms across human populations are understudied.
3. The interaction between SPM pathways and the endocannabinoid system is unexplored.
4. Biomarker development for clinical SPM measurement lags significantly behind mechanistic work.

CONCLUSION:
The SPM framework reframes neuroinflammation management from suppression to resolution. Simplex 2 will apply this framework specifically to postoperative delirium, where we hypothesize that perioperative SPM depletion is a primary driver of cognitive dysfunction.
