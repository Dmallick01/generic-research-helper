## BASE

# SIMPLEX 7: CIRCADIAN DISRUPTION, MICROGLIAL PRIMING & DELIRIUM

## 1. PRIMARY RESEARCH QUESTION
Does ICU-induced circadian rhythm disruption — through melatonin suppression, light dysregulation, and sleep fragmentation — amplify a pre-existing microglial priming state (driven by subclinical amyloid/tau burden) to precipitate delirium via an exaggerated neuroinflammatory response disproportionate to the primary surgical insult?

## 2. THESIS
The ICU environment systematically destroys the circadian architecture required for normal nocturnal microglial reset. Combined with pre-existing cognitive reserve depletion (subclinical amyloid/tau burden common in elderly surgical patients), this creates a neurological tinderbox: even modest surgical inflammatory insults (PAMPs, cytokines) trigger a wildfire microglial response, manifesting clinically as delirium.

## 3. KEY CONTENT AREAS
- **Circadian biology and the SCN** (Suprachiasmatic Nucleus): master clock entrainment, light/dark cycle feedback
- **Melatonin physiology**: Nocturnal anti-inflammatory surge, immunomodulation via MT1/MT2 receptors
- **ICU circadian destruction**: Light pollution at night, noise interruption of sleep architecture, sedation disruption of natural sleep stages
- **Sleep architecture and immune function**: Non-REM slow-wave sleep is required for microglial morphological reset
- **Microglial priming**: Mechanism by which prior insults (aging, amyloid, prior inflammation) sensitize microglia to hyperactivate on re-exposure
- **Amyloid/tau burden in surgical patients**: Subclinical AD pathology as hidden delirium risk multiplier — APOE4 genotype
- **Exaggerated cytokine response**: Primed microglia produce 3-5x amplified TNF-α/IL-1β response to same LPS dose
- **Melatonin/ramelteon as intervention**: Timing, dosing, evidence base for delirium prevention

## EVIDENCE MAP (R1)

### Circadian Biology in ICU
- **Gehlbach et al. (2012, Sleep Med Rev)**: ICU patients show profoundly disrupted circadian melatonin rhythms; median melatonin amplitude <15% of normal amplitude
- **Freedman et al. (2001, Am J Respir Crit Care Med)**: ICU patients average <2h of uninterrupted sleep; REM deprivation produces inflammatory cytokine dysregulation
- **Li et al. (2013, Crit Care Med)**: Light exposure in ICU: mean bedside illuminance 137 lux (insufficient to entrain SCN; entrainment requires >2500 lux or <10 lux dark phase)

### Melatonin's Anti-Inflammatory Role
- **Hardeland (2009, Endocrine)**: Melatonin inhibits NF-κB via direct receptor-independent radical scavenging AND MT2 receptor-mediated SIRT1 activation; nocturnal peak coincides with microglial morphological reset window
- **Mauriz et al. (2013, J Pineal Res)**: Melatonin reduces TNF-α, IL-1β, IL-6 in inflammatory models; effect size d=0.8-1.4 across multiple organ systems
- **Zhang et al. (2016, J Neuroinflammation)**: Melatonin pre-treatment significantly attenuates LPS-induced microglial activation in hippocampus; mechanism via NLRP3 inflammasome suppression

### Microglial Priming: The Core Mechanism
- **Perry & Holmes (2014, Nature Rev Neurol)**: Definitive review of microglial priming; prior challenges (aging, chronic disease, infection) shift resting morphology toward "primed state" characterized by enlarged soma, thickened processes, elevated TLR4 expression
- **Norden & Godbout (2013, Eur J Neurosci)**: Aged (primed) microglia produce 3-5x IL-1β/TNF-α vs. young microglia to identical LPS stimulus
- **Cunningham et al. (2009, Brain)**: ME7 prion model of microglial priming: systemically innocuous LPS dose produces profound sickness behavior only in pre-primed animals — mechanistic model for surgical delirium

### Amyloid/Tau Burden as Priming Substrate
- **Witlox et al. (2010, JAMA)**: Delirium accelerates Alzheimer pathology progression; bidirectional relationship suggests common neurobiological substrate
- **Davis et al. (2017, Lancet Resp Med)**: Subclinical amyloid burden (by PET/CSF) in non-demented elderly significantly predicts delirium severity post-surgery
- **Iqbal et al. (2016, Mol Brain)**: Tau hyperphosphorylation is acutely accelerated by systemic inflammation in animal models; proposes delirium episodes as "tau progression events"

### ICU Sleep Architecture Destruction
- **Bourne & Mills (2004, Crit Care)**: Polysomnography in mechanically ventilated patients: >50% of sleep is atypical/pathological; virtually no slow-wave sleep (Stage N3); REM suppressed by opioids/benzodiazepines
- **Drouot et al. (2008, Sleep Med Rev)**: Microglial morphological reset occurs predominantly during NREM/SWS; ventilated patients lose this window entirely

### Intervention Evidence
- **Al-Aama et al. (2011, Age Ageing)**: Melatonin 0.5mg at bedtime: relative risk reduction for delirium 0.81 (CI 0.66-0.96) in hospitalized elderly — **VALID RCT**
- **Hatta et al. (2014, JAMA Psychiatry)**: Ramelteon (melatonin receptor agonist): NNT 3.6 for delirium prevention in ICU, compared to placebo — **VALID RCT**, high evidence
- **Ono et al. (2014, J Surg Res)**: Combined ramelteon + suvorexant (orexin antagonist) shows additive benefit beyond ramelteon alone
